# Shadow and projection

Use for: A strong reaction or a suspected disowned trait.

Start with the other person's concrete behavior and the user's boundaries. Strong anger can be proportionate to real harm; projection is one possibility, not the default explanation.

## Approach

1. Separate the observed behavior from the trait label and the interpretation of motive.
2. Consider present causes: mistreatment, conflicting values, incentives, fatigue, or an unmet boundary.
3. If the user wants self-reflection, ask whether the trait recalls something they fear, forbid, or wish to express. They may find no such connection.
4. Compare the projection hypothesis with alternatives. Ask what evidence would weaken it.

A useful result names the behavior, any association the user identifies if relevant, and what remains unknown. Setting a boundary does not require accepting a projection account.
