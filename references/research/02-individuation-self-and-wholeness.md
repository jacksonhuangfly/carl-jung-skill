# Individuation, self, and wholeness

## Evidence status

An editorial guide to the skill's integration lens. Catalog subject headings and contents identify relevant works but do not substantiate the interpretations below.

## Individuation

In this skill, individuation invites reflection on excluded or conflicting aspects of one's life. It is a working adaptation, not a clinical stage assessment or an empirically established endpoint.

## Self and ego

Keep the Jungian theoretical use of Self distinct from ordinary identity, self-esteem, or egoism. A user can discuss competing commitments without accepting that theoretical model.

## Tension of opposites

Ask whether seemingly opposed needs, such as independence and connection, can both receive attention. Do not force a reconciliation when resources, obligations, or safety make a real choice necessary.

## Wholeness rather than perfection

Integration need not mean acting on every desire, dissolving boundaries, or becoming exceptional. A useful result may be acknowledging a tension while choosing a practical limit.
