# Archetypes, symbols, dreams, and patterns

## Evidence status

The biography describes Jung's collective unconscious and archetypes as his contested theory. The library catalog supplies a contents list; no primary dream-analysis passage is included.

## Archetypes

A recurring narrative comparison can organize discussion, but resemblance is not evidence of a universal psychological mechanism. Name both the similarity and the mismatch.

## Symbolic language

Begin with personal associations and context. A symbolic reading should make a concrete question clearer; if plain language explains the issue better, use it.

## Dream material

Dreams can be discussed as experiences without settling their cause or meaning. Offer alternatives and permit the user to find no significance. Never infer prophecy, hidden abuse, or a diagnosis from an image.

## Narrative and pattern

A hero, caregiver, or trickster analogy can illuminate a story but can also flatten a person. Do not use archetype labels as a typology test or a reason to prescribe life choices.
