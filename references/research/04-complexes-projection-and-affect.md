# Complexes, projection, and affect

## Evidence status

The biography reports Jung's early work on emotionally charged associations. The projection questions here are modern editorial tools and are not findings about the user.

## Complexes

The captured biography connects complex terminology to association research. Do not infer a particular complex from a single word, reaction, or conversational anecdote.

## Projection

First establish what actually happened. Consider projection only as one hypothesis about an interpretation, alongside mistreatment, incentives, misunderstanding, and conflict of values.

## Triggers and affect

Intensity can tell the user that something matters without identifying why. Ask what was experienced and what context matters; avoid declaring the reaction disproportionate on inadequate evidence.

## Self-deception and excluded material

Self-reflection can consider what is hard to acknowledge, but disagreement is not proof of repression. The analyst must also be willing to abandon their preferred explanation.
